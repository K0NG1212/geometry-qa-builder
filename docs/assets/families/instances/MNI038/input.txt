First-principles calculations map the band gap of lead-iodide perovskites APbI3 (corner-sharing PbI6 octahedra with a cation A in the cavities) across many real and hypothetical cations. The attached evidence.txt gives the evidence (facts E1–E4 from Filip et al., Nat. Commun. 5, 5757 (2014); numbers as reported in the paper). Which explanation of how the cation tunes the band gap is consistent with all of these facts?

选项：
A. Distortion of the PbI6 octahedra themselves dominates the gap.
B. Changes of the Pb-I bond length dominate the gap.
C. The orbitals of the cation form the band edges and set the gap directly.
D. The gap is set by the largest Pb-I-Pb bond angle, that is by the octahedral tilt; the cation acts only through its steric size, by controlling that tilt.

附件：evidence.txt（均为本题完整输入）。范围：证据取自论文，数值与观测按原文转述（不含原文段落）；只依据所给证据与通用科学知识作答，不需要也不允许查阅论文。 只回答一个字母 A、B、C 或 D。

===== evidence.txt =====
E1. Band structures computed with and without the cation agree near the gap; the cation states lie several eV away from the band edges.
E2. In the measured structures of methylammonium lead iodide the Pb-I bond lengths deviate by at most 0.1 per cent from their average of 3.18 A, and the diagonals of each octahedron are within 4 degrees of perpendicular.
E3. A model built from perfectly regular corner-sharing octahedra with no cation at all reproduces a gap that varies by up to about 1 eV with the Pb-I-Pb bond angles, following the largest of those angles.
E4. At fixed bond angles, the spread of Pb-I bond lengths among the realistic structures changes the gap by about 0.2 eV (roughly 3 eV per angstrom).
