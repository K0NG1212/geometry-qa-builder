Prismarenes are macrocycles built from 2,6-dialkoxynaphthalene units whose aromatic walls enclose a prism-shaped cavity lined with pi electron density, so they bind quaternary ammonium cations through C-H...pi contacts with the guest's methyl groups. The pentamers, prism[5]arenes, adopt an almost regular pentagonal prism (canting angles 90.0 +- 3.3 degrees, interior dihedral angles averaging 108 degrees, the ideal value) with a cavity that stays open, and a bound ammonium guest sits centred in it. The hexamers, prism[6]arenes, instead adopt a cuboid in which four of the alkyl chains turn inward and pack against the aromatic walls, filling the macrocycle's own cavity: the paper measures the resulting free cavity volume as 49 cubic angstroms for the ethoxy hexamer and 77 cubic angstroms for the propoxy hexamer, with the chains filling 90 percent and 84 percent of the available space respectively. Association constants with the dicationic guest N,N,N',N'-tetramethylpiperazonium were measured by competition NMR experiments in dichloromethane-d2 at 298 K, with errors below 15 percent. You need a host that binds the tetramethylpiperazonium dication essentially quantitatively at micromolar concentrations, so nothing below a billion per mole will do.
