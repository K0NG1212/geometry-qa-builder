The receptor is a polyaromatic capsule whose spherical cavity is encircled by aromatic panels and is virtually isolated from the surrounding water: unlike an open cleft, a closed cavity of this kind accepts a guest only if the guest's whole shape can be enclosed, so the paper uses it to recognise oligopeptides by the number and kind of their residues. The candidates are phenylalanine-containing dipeptides and their methyl-protected derivatives, all bound as 1:1 complexes. The paper reports that protecting the ends of the dipeptide with methyl groups strengthens binding, which it ascribes to additional hydrophobic CH-pi contacts with the aromatic panels, and that the free amino acid phenylalanine shows no detectable binding at all. Association constants were measured by isothermal titration calorimetry in water at 298 K. You need a guest that this capsule encloses especially tightly, above 3.5 hundred thousand per mole, so that the complex survives dilution. The attached candidates.txt describes four candidate structures, all of them prepared and measured in Shuto et al., JACS Au (2023). The candidate must satisfy: (affinity) the measured association constant in water is at least 3.5 × 10^5 M^-1. Exactly one of the four does so in the measured data, which is not shown to you. Which candidate guest should you choose?

===== candidates.txt =====
FF: the unprotected phenylalanine dipeptide — The parent guest: two phenyl side chains and free, charged termini at both ends, so no methyl groups are available for extra contacts with the panels and the polar ends remain exposed inside a cavity isolated from water.
FF-3Me: the phenylalanine dipeptide with methyl groups on both ends (C-terminal and N-terminal protection) — The same two phenyl side chains as the parent dipeptide, with methyl groups added at both the C-terminus and the N-terminus. The added methyl groups give further hydrophobic CH-pi contacts with the aromatic panels of the closed cavity, and the paper measures a 4.4-fold enhancement over the unprotected dipeptide.
FF-Me: the phenylalanine dipeptide with a methyl group on the C-terminus only — Protected at one end instead of two: one methyl group fewer than the answer, hence fewer added CH-pi contacts inside the cavity. The paper measures a 2.5-fold enhancement over the unprotected dipeptide.
WF: the tryptophan-phenylalanine dipeptide — One of the two phenyl side chains is replaced by the larger, bicyclic indole of tryptophan, so the guest's overall shape no longer matches the enclosed cavity as well; this is the guest the paper separates FF from in a competition experiment.
