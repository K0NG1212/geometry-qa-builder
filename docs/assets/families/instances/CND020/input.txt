Recognising hydrophilic carbohydrates in water is hard because water competes for every hydroxyl group. The receptor here, MPNT2+, is a water-soluble tetralactam macrocycle built from two dimethylnaphthalene panels joined by pyridinium spacers, giving a rigid preorganised cavity in which the two aromatic panels face each other and the lactam N-H groups point inward. A pyranose sugar bound in that cavity makes hydrogen bonds to the inward N-H donors and C-H...pi contacts between its ring C-H bonds and the naphthalene panels. The paper reports that the C-H bonds at the C2 and C5 positions make the largest contacts, that the C1 C-H bond makes none, and that the receptor prefers sugars whose ring substituents all sit in equatorial positions, since an axial substituent turns the corresponding C-H bond away from a panel. The receptor carries two positive charges, but the paper states that electrostatic attraction to an anionic substrate is by itself not enough to drive binding in water: the anionic sugar Neu5Ac shows no measurable binding at all. Association constants were measured in water by isothermal titration calorimetry and, independently, by NMR titration. You are building a sensor for one metabolite in aqueous solution and need a substrate that this receptor holds at least ten thousandfold above background, which is far above what it manages for ordinary monosaccharides. The attached candidates.txt describes four candidate structures, all of them prepared and measured in Zhai et al., ACS Cent. Sci. (2025). The candidate must satisfy: (affinity) the measured association constant in water is at least 1 × 10^4 M^-1. Exactly one of the four does so in the measured data, which is not shown to you. Which candidate substrate should you choose?

===== candidates.txt =====
Galactose: galactose, the C4 epimer of glucose — Differs from glucose only at C4, where the hydroxyl is inverted from equatorial to axial; that inversion turns the C4 C-H bond away from the naphthalene panel it would otherwise contact.
Glucose: glucose, the parent all-equatorial hexose — The same all-equatorial substitution pattern as glucuronic acid, with a neutral hydroxymethyl group at C6 instead of a carboxylate; the ring geometry and the orientation of every C-H bond are the same.
Glucuronic acid: glucuronic acid, the C6-carboxylate of glucose — A pyranose whose substituents are all equatorial, as in glucose, with the C6 hydroxymethyl group replaced by a carboxylate. The all-equatorial pattern leaves the C2 and C5 C-H bonds pointing at the aromatic panels.
Methyl alpha-glucoside: methyl alpha-glucoside, glucose with an alpha-linked methyl group at the anomeric carbon — Glucose with the C1 hydroxyl replaced by an alpha-linked methoxy group, which turns the C1 C-H bond from axial to equatorial. The paper notes that the C1 C-H bond makes no C-H...pi contact, so this flip is the least consequential of the ones compared, but the alpha linkage still alters the orientation of that bond.
