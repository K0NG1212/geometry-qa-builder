Galactose: galactose, the C4 epimer of glucose — Differs from glucose only at C4, where the hydroxyl is inverted from equatorial to axial; that inversion turns the C4 C-H bond away from the naphthalene panel it would otherwise contact.
Glucose: glucose, the parent all-equatorial hexose — The same all-equatorial substitution pattern as glucuronic acid, with a neutral hydroxymethyl group at C6 instead of a carboxylate; the ring geometry and the orientation of every C-H bond are the same.
Glucuronic acid: glucuronic acid, the C6-carboxylate of glucose — A pyranose whose substituents are all equatorial, as in glucose, with the C6 hydroxymethyl group replaced by a carboxylate. The all-equatorial pattern leaves the C2 and C5 C-H bonds pointing at the aromatic panels.
Methyl alpha-glucoside: methyl alpha-glucoside, glucose with an alpha-linked methyl group at the anomeric carbon — Glucose with the C1 hydroxyl replaced by an alpha-linked methoxy group, which turns the C1 C-H bond from axial to equatorial. The paper notes that the C1 C-H bond makes no C-H...pi contact, so this flip is the least consequential of the ones compared, but the alpha linkage still alters the orientation of that bond.
