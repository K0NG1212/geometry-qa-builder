A bisporphyrin molecular tweezer carries two zinc porphyrins on a common spacer. A ditopic guest with two nitrogen donors can coordinate one nitrogen to each zinc, so the tweezer binds it far more strongly than a single porphyrin does, but only if the separation between the two zinc atoms can match the distance between the guest's two nitrogens. The three tweezers below differ only in the spacer, and the paper reports how far each one can change its interporphyrin separation: the enediyne tweezer goes from a Zn-Zn distance of 4.9 angstrom in the free host to 7.0 angstrom in the complex, the stiff-stilbene tweezer only from 6.2 to 7.3 angstrom, and the glycoluril tweezer varies its interporphyrin distance through conformational changes of its seven-membered rings. The fourth candidate is a single zinc porphyrin, which has only one binding site. The guest here, DABCO (diazabicyclo[2.2.2]octane), is a rigid cage amine and, with 4,4'-bipyridyl, has the shortest nitrogen-nitrogen distance of the guests studied; being rigid, it cannot itself change shape to help the fit. Association constants were measured by UV-vis titration in dichloromethane at room temperature. You need to pick up this short, rigid diamine essentially completely at micromolar concentrations, so you need an association constant of at least a million per mole. The attached candidates.txt describes four candidate structures, all of them prepared and measured in Blom et al., Molecules (2015). The candidate must satisfy: (affinity) the measured association constant with DABCO is at least 1 × 10^6 M^-1. Exactly one of the four does so in the measured data, which is not shown to you. Which candidate host should you choose?

===== candidates.txt =====
Zn-TPP-single-porphyrin: Zn-TPP, a single zinc tetraphenylporphyrin with no spacer and no second porphyrin — One porphyrin, hence one zinc binding site: a ditopic guest can only coordinate at one end, so none of the cooperativity available to a two-site tweezer is possible. It serves as the reference host in the paper.
tweezer-1-enediyne: the enediyne tweezer, whose spacer is a planar enediyne — The planar enediyne spacer leaves the two zinc atoms only 4.9 angstrom apart in the free tweezer and 7.0 angstrom apart in the complex, the largest change of the three; the paper notes that this difference between the free and bound geometries leaves only a small preorganisation effect for the shortest guest.
tweezer-2-stiff-stilbene: the stiff-stilbene tweezer, whose spacer is a helical stiff stilbene — The least flexible of the three spacers: the paper reports that its Zn-Zn distance increases only slightly on binding, from 6.2 angstrom in the free tweezer to 7.3 angstrom in the complex, so it cannot vary its interporphyrin distance the way the enediyne and glycoluril tweezers can.
tweezer-3-glycoluril: the glycoluril tweezer, whose two porphyrins sit on a semi-rigid glycoluril spacer — The spacer is a glycoluril unit, and the paper reports that this tweezer can vary its interporphyrin distance through conformational changes of the seven-membered rings of the glycoluril backbone, so the separation of the two zinc atoms can adapt to the guest. The porphyrins are attached to the spacer by two covalent bonds each, which prevents lateral dislocation and porphyrin rotation but leaves twisting available.
