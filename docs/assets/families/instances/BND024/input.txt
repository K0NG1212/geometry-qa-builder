Bacteria scavenge iron with siderophores, and the periplasmic binding protein CeuE of Campylobacter jejuni captures the resulting Fe(III) complexes. The CeuE pocket completes the octahedral coordination of the iron with two of its own side chains, His227 and Tyr288, so a tetradentate siderophore only has to supply four of the six donors. The candidates are Fe(III) complexes of synthetic tetradentate bis-catecholamide ligands n-LICAM4-, in which the two catecholamide units are joined by a backbone linker of n atoms; the authors prepared n = 4, 5, 6 and 8 and measured the dissociation constant of each complex from wild-type CeuE by intrinsic fluorescence quenching, in triplicate, fitted to a 1:1 model. Co-crystal structures give the angle between the mean planes of the two catecholate rings as 97 degrees for the 4-atom linker and 111, 110 and 110 degrees for the 5-, 6- and 8-atom linkers, against an optimum of about 110 degrees; the paper states that the short butylene linker of the 4-atom ligand cannot reach that optimum, while the longer linkers can, at the cost of more conformational freedom. For reference, the natural hexadentate siderophore enterobactin carries a 5-atom backbone linker between its catecholamide groups. You need the analogue that the protein holds most tightly, so that it stays bound while the complex is handed on to the transporter. The attached candidates.txt describes four candidate structures, all of them prepared and measured in Wilde et al., Sci. Rep. (2017). The candidate must satisfy: (affinity) the measured dissociation constant from wild-type CeuE is at most 15 nM. Exactly one of the four does so in the measured data, which is not shown to you. Which candidate iron-siderophore complex should you choose?

===== candidates.txt =====
Fe-4-Lic: the Fe(III) complex of 4-LICAM4-, with a four-atom butylene linker — Four atoms between the two catecholamide units, the shortest of the series. In the co-crystal structure the angle between the catecholate mean planes is strained to 97 degrees instead of the optimal 110; the paper attributes this to the short butylene linker.
Fe-5-Lic: the Fe(III) complex of 5-LICAM4-, whose two catecholamide units are joined by a five-atom backbone linker — Five atoms between the two catecholamide units, the same linker length as in natural enterobactin. In the co-crystal structure the angle between the mean planes of the two catecholate rings is 111 degrees, at the optimum of about 110 degrees, and the linker is the shortest one that reaches it.
Fe-6-Lic: the Fe(III) complex of 6-LICAM4-, with a six-atom linker — Six atoms between the two catecholamide units. The angle between the catecholate mean planes is 110 degrees, the same as the optimum, so the iron coordination is unstrained; the extra atom adds conformational freedom to the linker.
Fe-8-Lic: the Fe(III) complex of 8-LICAM4-, with an eight-atom linker — Eight atoms between the two catecholamide units, the longest of the series. The angle between the catecholate mean planes is 110 degrees, identical to that of the six-atom ligand, so the paper ascribes its weaker binding to the larger number of conformational degrees of freedom in the longer linker rather than to the coordination geometry.
