ClO4-: perchlorate, the smallest tetrahedral anion of the four — Volume 54.7 cubic angstrom and packing coefficient 0.45, the smallest and loosest fit; the paper notes that smaller anions can form fewer simultaneous host-guest contacts.
IO4-: periodate, a tetrahedral anion — Volume 64.3 cubic angstrom and packing coefficient 0.52, almost the same size as perrhenate and likewise inside the favourable volume range.
ReO4-: perrhenate, a tetrahedral anion — Volume 64.8 cubic angstrom and packing coefficient 0.53, the closest of the four to the optimal 0.55 and sitting squarely in the 50 to 80 cubic angstrom range the paper identifies as favourable.
SbF6-: hexafluoroantimonate, an octahedral anion — Volume 81.8 cubic angstrom and packing coefficient 0.67, the largest of the four and above Rebek's optimal range. The paper reports that octahedral anions of this kind are locked in a fixed position inside the cavity, with four fluorine atoms pointing at the corners of the macrocycle's square belt and the remaining two at the opposite portals, and that host-guest C-H...F contacts hold that orientation.
