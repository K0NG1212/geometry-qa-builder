Scanning tunnelling spectroscopy maps the local density of states (LDOS) over one moiré cell of twisted bilayer graphene at 1.10°, at several gate dopings. The attached evidence.txt gives the evidence (facts E1–E5 from Kerelsky et al., Nature 572, 95 (2019); numbers as reported in the paper). Which explanation of the C3 symmetry breaking is consistent with all of these observations?

选项：
A. An asymmetric STM tip: the anisotropy is an imaging artefact that does not depend on the electronic states.
B. Electronic nematicity driven by the states at the Fermi level: strain only selects a direction, while the size of the C3 breaking follows the Fermi level and grows in the correlated state.
C. Heterostrain alone: a fixed lattice distortion breaks C3 by the same amount at every energy and doping.
D. A charge density wave that breaks translational symmetry has been established in these maps, and it causes the C3 breaking.

===== evidence.txt =====
E1. The samples carry a small uniaxial heterostrain (0.1-0.7 % across samples).
E2. LDOS maps at all energies between the two van Hove peaks break three-fold (C3) rotational symmetry to some extent.
E3. At each doping (about -1.2 × 10^12, 0 and +1.2 × 10^12 cm^-2) the strongest C3 breaking appears in the map taken at the Fermi level, so the most anisotropic energy moves with the gate.
E4. When the Fermi level sits on the conduction van Hove peak, where a 7.5 meV correlation gap opens, the maps around the gap break C3 more strongly than the same peak does at neutrality.
E5. The samples are not uniform over tens of moiré cells, so Fourier analysis of translational order was not possible.
