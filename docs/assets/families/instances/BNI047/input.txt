The potassium channel KcsA was trapped with its intracellular activation gate held open by an engineered disulfide bond, and crystallized carrying either of two mutations that change C-type inactivation: E71A, which removes it, and Y82A, which speeds it up. The attached evidence.txt gives the evidence (facts E1–E5 from Cuello et al., eLife 6, e28032 (2017); numbers as reported in the paper). Which account of deep C-type inactivation in KcsA is consistent with all of these observations?

选项：
A. Inactivation is the closing of the intracellular activation gate.
B. The collapsed filter is a crystallization artefact unrelated to inactivation.
C. Deep C-type inactivation is a collapse of the selectivity filter that happens while the activation gate stays open; a network of water molecules behind the filter stabilizes the collapsed conformation.
D. Inactivation comes from losing the potassium ion in the central cavity below the filter.

===== evidence.txt =====
E1. Both mutants are trapped with the same activation gate opening of 22 A (measured between Thr112 alpha-carbons on diagonal subunits).
E2. In single-channel recordings the E71A construct has a high open probability (0.8 to 0.9) and the Y82A construct a low one (0.015 to 0.04), both independent of pH.
E3. In the E71A structure (2.25 A) the selectivity filter holds four potassium ions in the usual 1,3 and 2,4 arrangement, and its backbone differs from the closed conductive state by only 0.19 to 0.26 A r.m.s.d.
E4. In the Y82A structure (2.4 A) the filter has lost the ions at sites 2 and 3, the Val76 carbonyl has flipped out of the axis, and the inter-subunit distance at Gly77 is reduced from 8 to 6 A.
E5. In the inactivated structure three ordered water molecules sit in a cavity behind the filter and hydrogen bond to the flipped Val76 carbonyl; they are absent from the conductive filter.
