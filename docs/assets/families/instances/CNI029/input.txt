The nematic E44 was doped with the chiral dopant R5011 (right-handed) or with its enantiomer S5011 (left-handed), and the mobile ions were measured by low-frequency dielectric spectroscopy. The attached evidence.txt gives the evidence (facts E1–E5 from Shaban et al., Materials 17, 5080 (2024); numbers as reported in the paper). Which explanation of the extra ions is consistent with all of these observations?

选项：
A. Any chiral dopant dissociates into ions in E44, whichever its handedness.
B. The right-handed helical order itself generates or traps the ions.
C. The ions come from the cell (alignment layers and electrodes), and the cell geometry controls them.
D. Impurity ions carried by this batch of R5011 (from manufacture or later contamination or degradation), unrelated to its handedness.

===== evidence.txt =====
E1. Adding R5011 (0 to 4 wt%) raises the mobile-ion density from 1.7 to 2.9 × 10^13 cm^-3, and the dielectric loss at 1 kHz grows linearly with R5011 content.
E2. Adding S5011 (0 to 4 wt%) leaves the ion density at 1.6-1.9 × 10^13 cm^-3; the spectra overlap those of pure E44.
E3. 1:1 mixtures of R5011 and S5011 (no net chirality) at 1, 2 and 4 wt% in total give ion densities and diffusivities close to those of R5011-only samples at 0.5, 1 and 2 wt%.
E4. Antiparallel (6 µm) and 90°-twisted (5 µm) cells with the same alignment layers and electrodes give the same ionic behaviour.
E5. The R- and S-doped mixtures (2.62 and 2.57 wt%) have nearly the same helical pitch (0.355 and 0.362 µm) and nearly identical reflection bands.
