Small-angle neutron scattering is measured on single crystals of the cubic chiral magnet MnSi (Tc = 29.5 K), with the neutron beam parallel to the applied magnetic field. The attached evidence.txt gives the evidence (facts E1–E5 from Mühlbauer et al., Science 323, 915 (2009); numbers as reported in the paper). Which interpretation of the A phase is consistent with all of these observations?

A. The conical phase, with the helix wave vector along the field.

===== evidence.txt =====
E1. In zero field MnSi orders as a helix of period about 190 A along the cubic <111> directions; the atomic lattice constant is 4.56 A.
E2. In the "A phase", just below Tc and in a field of about half the field Bc2 that fully polarizes the spins, six Bragg spots appear on a regular hexagon.
E3. With the sample rotated into many different orientations (the field always along the beam), the six spots always form a regular hexagon in the plane perpendicular to the field, whatever the orientation of the crystal axes.
E4. At lower temperature in the same field range (the conical phase), the spots lie along the field direction instead.
E5. Rocking scans give a magnetic mosaicity of about 1.75 degrees in the A phase, half the 3.5 degrees of the helical state, corresponding to a correlation length of at least 5500 A.
